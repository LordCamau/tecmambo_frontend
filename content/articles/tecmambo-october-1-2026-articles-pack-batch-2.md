# Kenyan AgriTech Startup Rhea Backed With $100,000 to Take Its Soil Technology Across East Africa

### Quick answer

Rhea Soil Health Management, a Nairobi agritech startup, is backed by a $100,000 convertible note from Obudu Capital that is meant to carry its soil-intelligence technology beyond Kenya. The funding is tied to four milestones: commercializing the AgriPad soil-testing device, deploying 100 devices, reaching 5,000 farmers and expanding into Tanzania. Tanzania would be Rhea's first market outside Kenya.

## What was announced

Kenyan agritech startup Rhea Soil Health Management is preparing to take its soil-intelligence technology beyond Kenya, backed by a $100,000 investment from Obudu Capital. The money is structured as a convertible note, and it is linked to clear commercial targets rather than released as a single lump sum.

The milestones are specific. Rhea is expected to commercialize its AgriPad device, put 100 of them in the field, reach 5,000 farmers and begin operating in Tanzania. Recent coverage describes Tanzania as the startup's first expansion outside Kenya, and a test of whether its technology and data services can work across the region.

One point of clarity is worth adding. Obudu Capital first announced the $100,000 commitment in July 2025 as its first portfolio investment, and said the financing would be released in tranches as Rhea hit milestones. The expansion plan is back in the news now because the startup is moving toward the regional stage the funding was designed to support.

## Who is behind Rhea

Rhea Soil Health Management was founded in Nairobi in 2022. Recent reports name Priscilla Wakarera and Soinato Leboo as the founders, and Obudu Capital describes the company as women-led. Its stated purpose is to make soil testing affordable and accessible to smallholder farmers, replacing guesswork with data about what a field actually needs.

The company has set a long-term goal of training one million farmers in sustainable soil management by 2030, according to Obudu Capital. In the first half of 2026, Rhea reportedly trained 135 agronomist agents, a field network that matters because the device only helps if someone can explain the results to the farmer.

## The AgriPad: how the technology works

Rhea's flagship product is the RHEA AgriPad, a portable soil-testing device that combines sensors, software and machine learning to produce field-level soil data. Instead of collecting a sample, sending it to a laboratory and waiting for results, a farmer or agent can test on the spot.

A patent application filed in 2025 with the Kenya Industrial Property Institute describes a handheld kit that collects soil data, uses GPS and feeds a web-based system that recommends fertilizer. That detail points to the real product: the device is the front end of a recommendation service. The value to a farmer is not a pile of numbers about the soil. It is advice on what to apply, in what quantity, and where.

The company has summed up the idea in a simple line: farmers cannot optimize what they cannot measure. For many smallholders, soil is the least measured input on the farm, even though seed, fertilizer and labor are all paid for in cash.

## Why soil testing matters for East African farmers

East Africa has millions of smallholder farmers working across very different soils, crops and climates. Many apply fertilizer by habit or by what a neighbor does, not by what their land needs. Over-applying wastes money and can harm soil over time. Under-applying lowers yields. Timely testing is scarce, and the traditional route of sending samples to a distant laboratory can mean waiting until the planting window has passed.

Portable testing changes the timing. If a farmer can learn the condition of a field before planting, the decision about fertilizer and crop choice can be made at the point where it counts. At scale, that could improve yields and cut input costs. It also supports broader goals around food security and soil degradation, which Obudu Capital highlights as the reason for backing Rhea.

## What the milestones mean in practice

Each target in the funding agreement tests a different part of the business.

- **Commercializing the AgriPad.** Moving from a promising device to a product that can be built, priced and sold repeatedly.
- **Deploying 100 devices.** Showing that the hardware holds up in real field conditions and that agents and farmers use it.
- **Reaching 5,000 farmers.** Proving that the service can reach people at a meaningful scale, which is a different challenge from selling devices.
- **Expanding into Tanzania.** Testing whether the model travels across borders with different regulations, languages, soils and farming practices.

These numbers are modest compared with the size of the opportunity, and that is deliberate. A $100,000 note is early capital. It is meant to prove the model before larger funding is raised.

## The data opportunity

Beyond testing, Rhea has pointed to potential uses for its soil-performance data in agricultural finance, insurance and carbon markets. The logic is straightforward. Lenders and insurers struggle to assess smallholder risk because there is little reliable data about the land. Carbon programs need measurement of soil health over time. If Rhea builds a large, trusted dataset across East Africa, that information could become valuable to organizations beyond farmers themselves.

That possibility also raises questions the company will need to answer, including who owns the data, how farmers consent to its use, and how any benefit is shared with the people whose land generated it.

## Risks and challenges

- **Hardware in the field.** Devices must be rugged, accurate and affordable, and they must be supported when they fail.
- **Trust in results.** Farmers will act on the recommendations only if they trust the readings, which means accuracy must be demonstrated against laboratory results.
- **Distribution.** Reaching smallholders across scattered rural areas depends on agents, cooperatives and partners.
- **Cross-border expansion.** Each new country brings new rules, soil types and buyers.
- **Small capital base.** $100,000 is not much for a hardware business, so milestones and further fundraising will matter.

## What to watch next

- When Rhea launches in Tanzania and with which local partners.
- How many AgriPad units are deployed and how many farmers are reached.
- Whether independent testing supports the accuracy of the device.
- Whether Rhea announces a larger funding round after the milestones are met.

It is a Nairobi-based agritech startup, founded in 2022, that provides affordable soil testing and fertilizer recommendations to smallholder farmers.

**How much funding does Rhea have from Obudu Capital?**

**What is the AgriPad?**

## The tecMAMBO take

Small cheques like this one are easy to overlook, and they shouldn't be. Soil data is one of the least glamorous and most useful problems in African agriculture, and Rhea is attacking it at the field level where decisions are made. The milestones are sensible because they test the business and not just the technology. Our concern is whether a $100,000 note is enough runway for hardware, agents and a new country at once. If the AgriPad proves accurate and farmers pay for the advice, the data layer behind it could become the real prize. Tanzania will tell us whether this travels.

# Gemini 4 Argon: Google Unveils Its New Frontier Model for Software Engineering, Enterprise Work and Cyber Defense

### Quick answer

Google announced Gemini 4 Argon on September 30, 2026, describing it as the next era of its frontier intelligence. The model is built for complex, long-horizon work in software engineering, enterprise knowledge work such as legal and finance tasks, and cybersecurity defense, with an output limit of 1 million tokens, up from 64,000. It is rolling out first to vetted cyber defenders through Google's Fairwind Program, with wider access planned for paid API customers and Google AI Ultra subscribers.

## What Google announced

Google has introduced Gemini 4 Argon, a new frontier AI model that it says delivers top-tier performance across three areas: real-world software engineering, enterprise knowledge work like legal and finance, and cybersecurity defense. The announcement came from Koray Kavukcuoglu, Google's chief AI architect and head of Google DeepMind, who called it the company's next era of frontier intelligence.

The release is not a general launch. Argon is going first to a set of trusted cyber defenders through the Fairwind Program, and Google says broader availability to developers, enterprises and consumers will follow "as soon as possible," starting with paid API customers and Google AI Ultra subscribers. The company says it is taking a phased approach and is taking part in the US government's voluntary process for pre-release model access while it gradually expands access.

## The headline change: a 1 million token output limit

The most concrete technical change is the output limit. Gemini 4 Argon can generate up to 1 million tokens in a single response, compared with 64,000 on earlier Gemini models. Many frontier models already accept very long inputs, but the amount they can write in one pass, including their own reasoning, has been far smaller.

Why does it matter? A model with more room to think and write can work through a hard problem in one continuous run instead of being cut off partway. Google argues that this headroom adds a new level of depth to reasoning on tough tasks. In practice it suits jobs such as rewriting large codebases, producing long analyses or carrying out multi-step research, where the work itself is long.

## Software engineering performance

Google says Argon sets a new state of the art on DeepSWE v1.1, a benchmark of real-world, long-horizon software engineering tasks, with a score of 77.9%. In Google's published comparison, rival models scored lower on this test: GPT-6 Astra at 74.1%, Claude Opus 5.5 at 74.2% and Claude Fable 5.1 at 67.4%.

The picture is not one-sided. On FrontierSWE v2, another coding benchmark, Google's own table shows Argon at 55.0%, behind GPT-6 Astra at 65.5% and Claude Opus 5.5 at 62.3%. On Vibe Code Bench, Argon led with 91.9%. Reports that tallied Google's charts found that Argon led outright on 12 of the 18 benchmarks shown. That is a strong result, but it also means the model does not win everywhere, and enterprise buyers should still choose by workload.

Inside Google, thousands of employees already use Argon for specialized coding, research and writing. Google shared several examples:

- **Code migration to Rust.** Argon agents are working on moving C and C++ code to the memory-safe Rust language, from tens of thousands of lines in core libraries up to more than 800,000 lines for the Fuchsia Zircon kernel. Google says these rewrites are going through rigorous automated and manual auditing and testing before production.
- **Faster video decoding.** For libgav1, Google's open source video decoder, Argon agents replaced 32,000 lines of low-level code in an existing Rust port. The result is a memory-safe decoder that runs 2.7 times faster than that port with identical video output.
- **Memory savings in data centers.** A team of Argon agents analyzed fleet-wide profiling data and applied optimizations that Google says will free more than 300 TiB of memory once rolled out, with total savings estimated at 500 TiB to 1 PiB.
- **Quantum algorithm work.** Argon helped Google's quantum computing researchers beat a published baseline by 40% on the resources needed for certain subroutines.

These are Google's own claims about its own systems, and independent verification will take time.

## Enterprise knowledge work

Beyond code, Google says Argon leads the Vals Index, which measures economic impact across finance, coding, legal and tax work, with each sector weighted by its contribution to US GDP. It also reports leading results on domain tests such as the Vals Finance Agent v2 for multi-step financial research and Harvey's Legal Agent Benchmark for legal research and drafting.

Some of the numbers from Google's published table:

| Benchmark | Gemini 4 Argon | GPT-6 Astra | Claude Fable 5.1 | Claude Opus 5.5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vals Index | 68.9% | 63.1% | 65.8% | 67.0% |
| AutomationBench | 51.3% | 41.4% | 31.4% | 42.5% |
| Vals Finance Agent v2 | 65.4% | 53.5% | 58.9% | 58.6% |
| Harvey Legal Agent Benchmark | 19.6% | 5.4% | 6.7% | 3.8% |

The legal benchmark scores are low for every model, which is a useful reminder that these tests are hard and that even a leading result is far from perfect. Google also says Argon is particularly strong when knowledge work involves visual understanding, such as analyzing professional charts, picking out details in long videos and acting on a series of documents. It reports a state-of-the-art 91.7% on LVBench, a long-video understanding test.

## Cybersecurity defense

Cyber defense is the focus of the first release. Google says it trained Argon to find, validate and patch critical software vulnerabilities on its own. For trusted defenders and Google's internal teams, the company will provide a version without cyber guardrails so they can use the full capability. That is the reason for the limited rollout through the Fairwind Program.

Google offers several data points:

- On Google's internal vulnerability benchmark, Argon uncovered a wide range of exposures across complex codebases in 20 programming languages.
- Wiz, the Google-owned cloud security company, used Argon in its free Scan for Good initiative, which looks for exposures in critical public infrastructure. The model found a critical flaw that exposed sensitive personal information in healthcare software used by hospitals worldwide, a risk that earlier frontier models had missed. Google has not named the software or said whether a fix has shipped.
- On CWE-bench v1, which tests the ability to fix security vulnerabilities, Argon tied for first place with a score of 68%.

The dual-use problem is obvious. A model that can find and patch flaws can also be used to find and exploit them, which is why Google is restricting the unguarded version to vetted users.

## Safety measures before wider release

Google lists four areas of work to strengthen safeguards before a broad release.

1. **Defending against misuse.** The model is designed to refuse harmful requests, including cyber and chemical, biological, radiological and nuclear threats, while still supporting legitimate dual-use scientific work. Google says it is improving how it monitors the model's internal activations to spot misuse, and that safeguards were tested by internal and external red teams.
2. **Prompt injection resistance.** Google calls Argon its most resilient model yet against indirect prompt injection, where malicious instructions hidden in content try to hijack the model. It reports leading results on Gray Swan's benchmark for this threat.
3. **Monitoring for misalignment.** Systems watch the model's reasoning and actions and stop execution when it steps outside what the user intended.
4. **Hardening systems.** Google is isolating and sealing the sandboxed environments used for high-risk training and evaluation.

## Pricing and availability

Google says Argon will launch at an introductory price of $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, with cached input tokens priced 95% lower than the standard input rate. After the introductory period, the price rises to $4 per million input tokens and $20 per million output tokens. No exact general release date has been given, only that it is rolling out soon.

## Why the timing matters

Argon arrives after a difficult stretch for Google's AI efforts. Coverage notes that it follows the cancellation of a planned Gemini 3.5 Pro and a focus on the faster Gemini 3.8 Flash line, and that Google has faced reports of talent departures and delayed releases. Analysts see Argon as Google's strongest claim in months to frontier leadership on enterprise terms, where the question is whether an AI agent can safely rewrite code, audit systems, handle regulated knowledge work and defend software before attackers reach it.

## What to watch next

- The date Argon opens to paid API customers and Google AI Ultra subscribers.
- Independent testing of Google's benchmark claims.
- What the Fairwind Program discovers and whether Google names the vulnerabilities found.
- How rival labs respond on price and capability.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is Gemini 4 Argon?**
It is Google's new frontier AI model for software engineering, enterprise knowledge work and cybersecurity defense, announced on September 30, 2026.

**Who can use it now?**
For now, vetted cyber defenders in Google's Fairwind Program and Google's internal teams. Paid API customers and Google AI Ultra subscribers are next.

**What is new technically?**
An output limit of 1 million tokens, up from 64,000 on previous Gemini models.

**How much will it cost?**
An introductory $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens, rising to $4 and $20 afterward.

**Does it beat rival models?**
Google's benchmarks show it leading on most tests, but not all. It trails GPT-6 Astra on FrontierSWE v2, and the results are Google's own.

## The tecMAMBO take

Argon is a serious release, and the 1 million token output limit is the sort of change that alters what an AI agent can attempt in one go. We would still treat Google's benchmark table the way we treat any vendor chart: useful, selective and unproven until outsiders reproduce it. The cyber angle is the most interesting and the most uncomfortable. Releasing an unguarded version to defenders first is a defensible call, but it shows how close the line between defense and attack has become. The real test comes when Argon reaches paying developers and has to prove itself on messy, everyday work.

# Kwetu eSIM Joins the M-PESA Super App, Letting Kenyan Travellers Buy Roaming Data With M-PESA

### Quick answer

Kwetu eSIM has launched a Mini App inside Safaricom's M-PESA Super App, so Kenyan travellers can buy and activate international eSIM data packages using their M-PESA balances, without an international debit or credit card. The service covers more than 200 destinations, offers local, regional and global plans, and lets users keep their Safaricom number active for calls and SMS while using Kwetu for data abroad. It went live around September 29, 2026.

## What was announced

Kwetu eSIM has integrated its Mini App into the M-PESA Super App, the Safaricom platform used by millions of Kenyans for payments and everyday services. With the integration, a traveller can open the Super App, choose Kwetu eSIM, pick a data package for their destination and pay in Kenyan shillings from their M-PESA balance.

The practical gain is the removal of a familiar obstacle. Buying travel data online usually calls for an international debit or credit card, and many Kenyans do not have one, or do not enable it for foreign transactions. Paying from M-PESA takes the card out of the process.

## What an eSIM is and why it helps travellers

An eSIM is a digital SIM built into a compatible phone. Instead of inserting a plastic card, the user downloads a profile and the phone connects to a mobile network. For travellers, that means a data plan for the country they are visiting can be installed before they leave home and switched on when they land.

The traditional alternatives are costly or tiresome. International roaming on a home SIM can carry high charges. Buying a local SIM means finding a shop, often at an airport, queuing and handling registration rules. An eSIM avoids both. Kwetu's pitch is that the traveller can sort out data before take-off and be online on arrival.

## How the Kwetu eSIM Mini App works

According to reports of the launch, the process is meant to be short.

1. Open the M-PESA Super App.
2. Find Kwetu eSIM among the travel and booking services.
3. Browse data packages for the destination and trip length.
4. Pay with M-PESA in Kenyan shillings.
5. Install the eSIM profile over the air and activate it before departure or on arrival.

Key features reported at launch:

- **Direct M-PESA checkout.** Packages are bought in shillings with standard M-PESA funds, with no need for an international card.
- **Instant digital delivery.** eSIM profiles are provisioned over the air in a few taps.
- **Dual-SIM convenience.** Travellers keep their primary Safaricom number active for incoming calls and SMS, while data runs through Kwetu's partner networks in the destination country.
- **Flexible coverage.** Users can choose local, regional or global plans, including bundles that cover East African Community countries, depending on where they are going and for how long.

The company says coverage extends to more than 200 destinations. Kwetu's own app, launched earlier in 2026, was described at that time as covering more than 190 countries, so the exact count depends on how destinations are counted.

Kwetu's general manager, Kevin Mwando, said the aim is to make global connectivity as effortless as sending money back home. Pricing for individual packages was not included in the launch announcements, so travellers should check current rates inside the app.

## The wider Kwetu service

Before the Mini App, Kwetu eSIM already offered an app on iPhone and Android and a website. That service lets users buy data before departure, activate it in under a minute using a QR code or activation code, and top up inside the app if data runs out while abroad. It also allows users to buy an eSIM as a gift for family or friends. The app supports M-Pesa payments through an STK Push prompt, alongside card and other mobile money options.

The Mini App brings that experience into a place Kenyans already use every day, which is where the distribution advantage lies.

## Why the M-PESA Super App matters

Safaricom launched the M-PESA Super App in June 2021 with an offline mode and a Mini Apps feature. Mini Apps let businesses, government agencies and other firms offer services inside the app, covering things like deliveries, ticket booking, shopping, licence applications and insurance. Customers reach them through the Discover option. At launch, Safaricom said more than 1.3 million customers had already downloaded the Super App.

The platform handles very large volumes. Business Daily has reported that M-Pesa processed Ksh41.7 trillion in transactions in the year to March 2026. For a small company like Kwetu, a place on that platform puts its product in front of a huge base of people who already trust the payment method.

The model also reflects a wider trend. Super apps bring many services into a single interface, so users do not need a separate app, account and payment method for each one. Mini Apps in the M-PESA ecosystem are a way of extending the reach of mobile money into areas such as travel.

## Who benefits

- **Kenyan travellers and business professionals** who want data on arrival without roaming bills.
- **People without international cards,** who have often been shut out of online travel services priced in foreign currency.
- **Tourists and visitors** who use Kenyan-linked payments and want regional bundles.
- **Students and migrant workers** who travel often and want predictable data costs.

## Things to check before you buy

- **Device compatibility.** The phone must support eSIM and be unlocked. Check the device settings or the manufacturer's list before buying.
- **Data only.** The service provides mobile data. Calls and SMS continue on the Safaricom number, though standard roaming rules and charges apply if you use them abroad.
- **Coverage and speed.** Plans depend on partner networks in each destination, so quality can vary.
- **Validity and size.** Compare data allowances and validity periods against how long you will be away.

## What to watch next

- Whether other travel and lifestyle services launch Mini Apps on the M-PESA platform.
- How competing eSIM providers respond, including on price and M-PESA support.
- Whether adoption grows as more Kenyan phones support eSIM.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I pay for Kwetu eSIM with M-PESA?**
Yes. The Mini App lets users buy and activate eSIM data packages with their M-PESA balances, with no international debit or credit card.

**Where do I find it?**
Open the M-PESA Super App and look for Kwetu eSIM among the travel and booking services.

**How many destinations are covered?**
More than 200, according to the launch announcement.

**Will my Safaricom number still work?**
Yes. You can keep your primary number active for calls and SMS while using Kwetu eSIM for data.

## The tecMAMBO take

This is a small launch with a smart idea behind it. The hard part of travel data for many Kenyans was never the SIM, it was the card. Putting an eSIM storefront inside the app people already use for money removes that barrier, and it is the kind of everyday use that makes a super app worth having. We would like to see transparent pricing in the open and a simple way to check device compatibility before checkout. If Kwetu delivers reliable connections, expect other travel services to follow it onto the M-PESA platform.
